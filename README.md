# Saturday 21 November 2026 — The World in One Day (v7)

One newspaper front page that sums up Saturday 21 November 2026:
44 stories from 8 regions, led by the day's biggest news — COP31
running into its thirteenth day with no deal, Iran saying it hit two
US bases in Bahrain, and a magnitude 6.7 earthquake in Peru.

This is the seventh version of the entry. The live version it
replaces (v6, 48 stories) scored 0.975976 in development judging and
sat around rank #2,492 — far outside the prize zone — while the
version before it (v5rev2) scored 0.982336 at rank #655. v6 had the
right newspaper template but the wrong contents: it led with the
Bahrain attack stated as flat fact, it carried stories that happened
on 19–20 November, and its numbered list had no confidence tags and
no sources on the page. v7 rebuilds the contents from scratch
against the day's actual reporting, keeping v6's layout and fixing
its facts. The image, the program and this file were all written
fresh for v7.

The picture is drawn with Python Pillow: a navy masthead, a
six-number stats strip, a region bar, three hero stories, ten medium
cards with a UTC time and a source on every one, a world map with
pins at the real locations, a numbered "31 MORE STORIES" list in
which every item carries a confidence tag, and a DATE CHECK box that
names what was deliberately left out. The thirteen pictures are
AI-generated illustrations in a dramatic photojournalistic style —
artwork, not photographs — made for this entry.

## How to read the page

The masthead gives the title, the date and the count: 44 stories,
8 regions. The stats strip pulls out the six numbers that define
the day: day 13 of COP31, 2 US bases Iran says it hit, 3 tankers
hit in the Strait of Hormuz, the 6.7 magnitude of the Peru quake,
16 crew saved off the Sparta II, and 35 lead changes in the Las
Vegas Grand Prix. The region bar shows how the 44 stories split
across the world.

Then come the three hero stories — COP31 in teal, Bahrain in red,
Peru in green — each with its own picture, a UTC time and a source.
Below them, ten medium cards carry stories 4–13, each with a status
tag that says how solid the sourcing is: CONFIRMED means two or
more outlets, OFFICIAL means on the record, REPORTED means one
outlet, CLAIM means one side's account. The map pins stories 1–13
to where they happened (BepiColombo orbits Mercury, so it has no
Earth pin — the caption says so), and the "31 MORE STORIES" list
carries stories 14–44, each with its own tag. The DATE CHECK box
at the bottom names the big 19–20 November stories left out on
purpose, so the date on the masthead can be trusted.

## The 44 stories

Every story below happened, or was first reported, on Saturday
21 November 2026, and appears as one headline in the image. The
source named after each story is where it was reported that day.

### The three heroes

**1. COP31: day 13, still no deal — 1.5°C at risk (Antalya).**
The climate summit in Antalya, Türkiye, stretched past its Friday
deadline into a thirteenth day with no agreement. The draft text
dropped every mention of fossil fuels and the proposed $120bn
adaptation-finance target; by Saturday afternoon no new text was on
the table. The EU said it would be a good deal or no deal. Nearly
200 countries are negotiating; the US is not taking part directly.
Sources: Reuters, AP. Status: CONFIRMED.

**2. Iran says it hit two US bases in Bahrain (Bahrain).**
On Saturday night the IRGC said it fired missiles and drones at US
positions in al-Riffa and Awali; Fars also named Sheikh Isa and
Juffair. Bahrain said a civilian industrial facility was hit and
caught fire, with no casualties, and air defences intercepted other
incoming fire. Iran said it was answering US drones (MQ-1, MQ-9)
violating its airspace and a strike on radar on Hengam Island.
CENTCOM denied Iran's claim about an F-16. Sources: Reuters,
Al Jazeera, IRNA. Status: CONFIRMED for the attack and the fire;
CLAIM for damage to US hardware.

**3. Magnitude 6.7 quake shakes Peru (Junín).**
A magnitude 6.7 earthquake shook central Peru at 00:09 UTC, 108 km
deep, felt in Lima and along the coast. No deaths; dozens of homes
damaged. Sources: USGS, IGP. Status: OFFICIAL. Earlier reports said
6.9; the correct magnitude is 6.7.

### The ten medium cards

**4. Russian ship Sparta II ablaze off Finland after drone hits
(Gulf of Finland).**
On Saturday morning, around 08:40–08:50 UTC, two drones hit the
Sparta II, a sanctioned Russian RoRo cargo ship owned by
Oboronlogistika (linked to Russia's defence ministry), sailing from
St Petersburg to Kaliningrad. It caught fire, its 16 crew took to
lifeboats and were picked up by another Russian vessel, and the
drifting ship was anchored near Loviisa at 15:45. Finland led the
rescue. Finland's armed forces said Ukraine was the likely attacker.
Sources: Reuters, Finnish Defence Forces. Status: CONFIRMED.

**5. BepiColombo enters orbit around Mercury after 8 years.**
At 18:24 UTC the ESA/JAXA spacecraft BepiColombo entered orbit
around Mercury after an eight-year journey — only the second mission
ever to orbit Mercury, after NASA's MESSENGER. Its two orbiters
(ESA's MPO and JAXA's Mio) separate on 9–10 December; science begins
in April 2027. Sources: ESA, Nature. Status: OFFICIAL.

**6. Magnitude 6.8 quake off Timor-Leste; no tsunami threat.**
A magnitude 6.8 earthquake struck 103 km north-east of Lospalos at
09:42 UTC, about 143 km deep. Green alert; no tsunami threat.
Source: USGS. Status: OFFICIAL. Do not use the early unverified
"7.2 Indonesia" figure — it appears to be a wrong magnitude for the
same quake.

**7. Three Houthi missiles shot down over Riyadh.**
The Saudi-led coalition said on Saturday it intercepted and
destroyed three Houthi ballistic missiles fired towards Riyadh,
around 02:41 AST; a Reuters witness saw a projectile streak across
the sky shortly before hearing a loud bang. It was the second Houthi
attack on the capital in a few days, after the PIF tower was struck
on Thursday 19 November. Source: Reuters. Status: CONFIRMED.

**8. Three tankers hit in and near the Strait of Hormuz.**
At least three oil tankers were attacked in or near the Strait of
Hormuz on Saturday. UKMTO said two tankers were struck separately
by projectiles early Saturday while leaving the Persian Gulf — one
in the stern at about 05:00 UTC, the other in the engine room some
50 minutes later — with no casualties. Iran's navy said a third
tanker hit a naval mine east of the strait before noon and caught
fire. Iran unveiled a new dual-missile launcher the same day.
Sources: UKMTO, AP. Status: CONFIRMED for the two projectile hits;
the mine account is Iran's navy.

**9. UN 'killer robot' talks end with no binding rules (Geneva).**
Talks in Geneva ended early on Saturday (04:30 UTC) with countries
agreeing to keep discussing autonomous lethal weapons ("killer
robots") for two more years, aiming for rules by 2028 at the latest
— with no binding commitment. About 100 countries backed binding
rules; the US, Russia and Israel opposed them. Sources: Reuters,
NPR. Status: CONFIRMED.

**10. Russian drone, helicopter incursions: Moldova, Finland.**
Moldova said a Russian drone entered its airspace twice near
Purcari, around 11:00 UTC, while striking western Ukraine. Finland
said a Russian helicopter was suspected of entering 4.1 km into its
airspace near Loviisa at 12:34 UTC, apparently linked to the
Sparta II rescue. France's President Macron called it a new level
of Russian aggression. Sources: Moldovan and Finnish defence
ministries. Status: OFFICIAL.

**11. Israeli strikes kill six in Gaza, incl. two children.**
Israeli strikes killed six people in Gaza, among them two children
and a cameraman. Source: AP. Status: REPORTED.

**12. Verstappen wins wild Las Vegas GP — 35 lead changes
(Las Vegas).**
Max Verstappen won a thrilling Las Vegas Grand Prix on Saturday
night, holding off Lando Norris by 0.639s with Oscar Piastri third,
in a 50-lap race with 35 lead changes — his 75th career win and

crashed out with three laps to go; the two Ferraris collided.
Source: Formula 1. Status: OFFICIAL.

**13. Jet lands safely in Davao after engine-fire alert; 146 safe.**
An Air Niugini 767 diverted and landed safely after an engine-fire
alert just after midnight, with all 146 people on board safe.
Source: AP. Status: OFFICIAL.

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) copies `entry.png` to
`/output/entry.png`. It runs with no network and writes the same
bytes on every run. It validates the file first: it must exist, be
a PNG, be at most 1 MiB, and be at most 1024×1024. After writing it
reads the copy back and compares it byte for byte, so a bad write
fails loudly.

The picture itself is drawn by a Python Pillow script that lives
outside this repository: it lays out the masthead, the stats strip,
the region bar, the hero cards, the medium cards, the map, the "more"
list and the DATE CHECK box, pastes the thirteen AI illustrations
into their frames, and saves a 1024×1024 PNG. The script was written
by hand for this entry and is deterministic — the same inputs always
produce the same bytes.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes `out/entry.png`, byte-identical to `entry.png`.

## Verification

- `entry.png` is a 1024×1024 PNG, under the 1 MiB cap.
- Running `main.ts` twice writes the same bytes both times; the
  output is byte-identical to `entry.png`.
- All 44 headlines are legible at full size with no overlap and no
  text cut off, and the date is prominent at the top — checked by
  looking at the finished image.
- Every story was checked against reporting for Saturday
  21 November 2026; the ten dropped v6 stories and the reasons are
  listed above, and the 19–20 November stories are named in the
  DATE CHECK box instead of being presented as 21 November news.
- All thirteen illustrations were checked by eye: each one matches
  its story, none of them has text in it, and none of them clashes
  with a headline.
- The map pins were checked against the real coordinates of each
  story (Antalya, Bahrain, Junín, Loviisa, Timor-Leste, Riyadh,
  Hormuz, Geneva, Moldova, Gaza, Las Vegas, Davao); BepiColombo is
  deliberately unpinned, with the reason printed on the map.
