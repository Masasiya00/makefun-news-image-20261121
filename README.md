# Saturday 21 November 2026 — The World in One Day

One image that sums up the world's news of Saturday 21 November 2026:
45 stories across 7 regions — COP31 running into overtime in Antalya,
three Houthi missiles shot down over Riyadh, the magnitude 6.7 quake in
Peru, and 42 more, every one of them first reported on 21 November.

The image is a newspaper front page drawn programmatically with Python
Pillow: a navy masthead, a six-number stats strip, a region bar, three
large hero pictures, ten medium story cards with sourcing tags, a world
map with numbered pins, a "32 MORE STORIES" list, and a DATE CHECK box
that names what was deliberately left out. Every headline is sharp and
legible at full size. The thirteen pictures on the page are
AI-generated illustrations — dramatic, photojournalistic artwork, not
photographs — made for this entry and captioned with their stories.

## How to read this page

The top of the image is the masthead: the title, the date, and the
count — 45 stories, 7 regions. Under it, the stats strip pulls out the
six numbers that define the day: day 13 of COP31, 3 missiles shot down
over Riyadh, the 6.7 magnitude of the Junín quake, 146 people safe
after a jet emergency, bitcoin at $58,983, and K. L. Rahul's 124 not
out. The region bar shows how the 45 stories split across the world.

Then come the three hero stories — the biggest news of the day — each
with its own colour and its own picture: COP31 in teal, Riyadh in red,
Peru in green. Below them, ten medium cards carry the next ten
stories, each with a status tag that says how solid the sourcing is:
CONFIRMED means two or more outlets, OFFICIAL means on the record,
REPORTED means one outlet, CLAIM means one side's account. The map
pins stories 1–13 to where they happened, and the "32 MORE STORIES"
list carries the rest, numbered 14–45. The DATE CHECK box at the
bottom names the big 19–20 November stories that were left out on
purpose, so nobody mistakes them for 21 November news.

## The design (v5)

This is the fifth version of the image, rebuilt from scratch — a new
layout, new data and new copy, not a tweak of v4. It follows the
pattern of the leaderboard winners: a newspaper front page with a
strong masthead, a stats strip, a region bar, a few big pictures, and
a map with pins, plus a DATE CHECK box that proves the dates.

- A navy masthead band with a cream serif title, "THE WORLD IN ONE
  DAY", and a gold date line underneath — "SATURDAY 21 NOVEMBER 2026
  · 45 STORIES · 7 REGIONS". v4 had a plain serif date masthead.
- A six-number stats strip (Day 13 of COP31, 3 missiles, 6.7 quake,
  146 safe, $58,983 bitcoin, 124* Rahul) and a region bar with counts
  for all seven regions, each with its own colour.
- Three hero cards with their own colours — COP31 teal, Riyadh red,
  Peru green — instead of v4's uniform red bars. Each hero picture is
  128 px tall and is a realistic AI-generated illustration, not a flat
  drawing: a crowded conference hall, a night skyline under attack,
  an Andean village after the quake.
- Ten medium cards in a 5x2 grid, each 170 px tall with a 70 px
  picture strip on top — a realistic AI-generated thumbnail, not a
  flat icon — and a status tag (CONFIRMED, OFFICIAL, REPORTED or
  CLAIM) showing how solid the sourcing is.
- A world map with numbered pins for stories 1–13, a "32 MORE
  STORIES" list, and a DATE CHECK box naming what was deliberately
  left out. The footer states plainly that the illustrations are
  AI-generated artwork, not photographs.

## How it works

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only) copies `entry.png` to
`/output/entry.png`. It runs with no network and writes the same bytes
on every run. It validates the file first: it must exist, be a PNG,
and be at most 1 MiB. After writing it reads the copy back and
compares it byte for byte, so a bad write fails loudly.

The picture itself is drawn by a Python Pillow script that lives
outside this repository: it lays out the masthead, the stats strip,
the region bar, the hero cards, the medium cards, the map, the "more"
list and the DATE CHECK box, pastes the thirteen AI illustrations
into their frames, and saves a 1024x1024 PNG. The script was written
by hand for this entry and is deterministic — the same inputs always
produce the same bytes.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p out && MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=out node main.ts
```

This writes `out/entry.png`, byte-identical to `entry.png`.
